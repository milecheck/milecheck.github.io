Why would a Geotab customer need a mile marker when the truck has GPS?

GPS tells the computer where the truck is. The people who respond to it work from the road and the mile.

  • Phones don't close the gap. The FCC requires carriers to locate only 80 percent of wireless 911 calls within 50 meters (FCC). Virginia DOT tells drivers to give the mile marker sign when they call 911 (VDOT).
  • Highway records are kept by milepost. State crash reports, work zones and DOT alerts are all located by route and mile. A fleet that logs incidents the same way can match them.
  • Why it matters to Geotab. Every customer with trucks on highways gets asked "what mile marker?" Today the dispatcher calls the driver to find out. MileCheck answers it in MyGeotab for every vehicle, and Geotab sells it.

1. Where is the truck, in words a dispatcher can use?

Live todayMyGeotab Add-In and Geotab Drive

When someone calls 911 for a driver, dispatch asks first: what road, which direction, what mile marker? "I-84 westbound, mile 300" gets a crew there. Reading GPS coordinates over the phone is slow and easy to get wrong, and a dispatcher or fleet manager calling for the driver can't share the truck's location from their own phone. Pick the vehicle in MyGeotab and MileCheck shows that line, so help arrives sooner and the truck is back on the road sooner.

Apple's satellite SOS works the same way. When it asks where you are, its own example is "near mile marker 43."

The Add-In lists the nearest crossings with the wait for commercial, FAST and passenger lanes. It shows both directions. Into the US comes from US Customs and Border Protection. Into Canada comes from the Canada Border Services Agency. Each card shows when the agency last updated it.

The same data by API. /v1/borders returns every US land crossing on the Canada and Mexico borders (85 ports) with lane waits, and can filter to the crossings near a point. This is the endpoint your team asked about.

How do DOT crews and EMS use mile markers?

  • DOT maintenance. Plow routes, sign repairs, guardrail hits and pothole work are logged by road and mile marker. A DOT fleet on Geotab could have every truck's position recorded that way automatically.
  • 911 and EMS dispatch. The first thing dispatch asks a highway caller is the nearest mile marker. Fire and ambulance crews are sent to a road, direction and mile, not to coordinates.
  • State patrol. Crash reports are filed by route and milepost, so an officer's location has to match that system.
