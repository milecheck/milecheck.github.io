Why this matters more than it seems

Tire pressure drops roughly 1 PSI for every 10-degree drop in temperature — a tire that read fine in October can be noticeably low by the first hard freeze, and low pressure shows up as worse traction exactly when you need the most grip. Check pressure at the start of the season and again after any sharp cold snap. Questions, answered

Why does tire pressure matter more in cold weather?

Tire pressure drops as the temperature falls — roughly 1 PSI for every 10-degree drop. A tire that was fine in October can be noticeably underinflated by the first hard freeze.

Do I need an inflator with an auto shut-off?

It's worth having. Set your target PSI, and the inflator stops on its own instead of you standing there watching a gauge, which also avoids overinflating a tire past its rated pressure.

Can a portable inflator handle a flat tire, or just low pressure?

It can get a slow leak or an underinflated tire back to pressure, but it won't fix a puncture or a tire that's fully flat. Pair it with a tire plug kit or sealant for actual punctures.
