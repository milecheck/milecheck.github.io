Tire chains for mountain passes

By wheel-well clearance and how easy they go on. Read the guide.

Tight clearance

Security Chain Company SZ143 Super Z6 cable tire chain

Built for cars, pickups, and SUVs with limited clearance around the tire. It's a cable chain, not a link chain, so it sits lower against the tire — the difference that matters if your last set rubbed the wheel well. Installs without needing to move the vehicle first, which is the part that actually saves time on a cold shoulder.

Easiest install

König XG-12 Pro

A self-tensioning design, so there's no crawling back under the car to re-tighten once you've started driving — the step that trips up most people the first time they chain up. Costs more than a basic cable chain, which is the tradeoff for not fighting with it in the cold.

Budget

Glacier 1038 cable tire chain

A basic cable chain for a car that sees snow a handful of times a season, not a car that lives on a mountain route all winter. Covers the same "chains required" checkpoint as the pricier options — it just asks a little more patience to put on.

1,000 peak amps, spark-proof reverse-polarity protection, and about the size and weight of a small hardcover book. Covers gas engines up to roughly 6.0 liters and diesel engines up to 3.0 liters — the range most passenger vehicles fall into.

Budget

GOOLOO GP2000

Enough power for small-to-midsize passenger cars and SUVs at a lower price point than the premium options. A solid choice if your car is the only reason you need one.

Trucks and diesels

NOCO Boost HD GB70

2,000 peak amps — the step up from the GB40 for engines a standard jump starter isn't rated to handle. Worth the extra size and weight if you drive something bigger than a passenger car.
