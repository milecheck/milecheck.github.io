What this page can and cannot tell you

A plow pin is where a UDOT truck was when it last reported, with the route and mile the DOT attached. It says a truck was there. It does not say the road behind it is bare, and it does not say when the next pass comes. The DOT's camera, where there is one on SR-158, SR-39, SR-162, shows the surface itself. The closure list is UDOT Traffic's own feed, read every five minutes and converted to the format MileCheck uses for every state. A quiet list means UDOT Traffic has posted nothing for these roads, which is different from a road being open.

The 15 miles ring is the search area for trucks and cameras. A truck reporting on SR-158, SR-39, SR-162 is kept out to 45 miles from the base, the stretch a driver is actually on; anything inside the ring is kept whatever road it is on, so a truck on a side road near the base still shows. The resort's own Powder Mountain current conditions is the source for snowfall, lifts and the parking lots.

Questions about the road to Powder Mountain

Is the road to Powder Mountain open right now?

The map shows every closure and crash UDOT Traffic is publishing on SR-158, SR-39, SR-162 as a red or orange marker, and the list under it says when the DOT last updated each one. No marker means UDOT Traffic has not posted a closure, not that the road is open. It reloads every five minutes. For the official word, open UDOT Traffic.

Where are the plows on the road to Powder Mountain?

On the map, as blue truck pins. Each is a UDOT truck's last reported GPS position, with the route and mile the DOT snapped it to, how long ago it reported, and the material it is spreading where the DOT sends that. A pin tells you a truck was there at that moment. It does not tell you the lane behind it is bare. Trucks that have not reported in an hour are faded and say so.

Are chains required on the road to Powder Mountain?

Chain and traction requirements are set by UDOT per road and per storm, and they show up in the UDOT Traffic feed as restrictions, which this page lists when they are on SR-158, SR-39, SR-162. Utah's chain law explains the levels and who they apply to. Always follow the posted signs.

How often does this page update?

Plow positions every minute, closures and crashes every five minutes, the camera list every ten. MileCheck's server reads the UDOT and UDOT Traffic feeds and converts them to one format. Each truck and each alert carries its own time stamp from the DOT, shown next to it.
