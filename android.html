What it does

One number. Exactly where you are.

01

Real-time mile marker — to one decimal place

MileCheck reads your GPS position and shows your nearest highway mile marker, interpolated between physical signs. If you're between MP 47 and MP 48, you see 47.3 — not just "somewhere around 47." The app displays your route, direction, and speed alongside the milepost.

02

All 50 states, no signal required

The full milepost dataset — every interstate, US highway, and most state routes across all 50 states — ships bundled inside the Android app. There's nothing to download. There's no setup. When you enter a canyon, a tunnel, or a dead zone, the mile marker keeps working from the last known GPS fix.

03

Live DOT road alerts in 31 states

Closures, crashes, construction zones, weather conditions, and chain requirements — pulled directly from state DOT APIs and updated every few minutes. Filter by type (closure only, crash only), by route, or by distance from your current position. Available in: AK, AR, AZ, CO, CT, DE, FL, GA, IA, ID, IL, IN, KS, LA, MA, ME, MN, MT, NC, NE, NH, NV, NY, OH, OR, SC, TX, UT, VT, WA, WI.

04

Trip logging — opt-in, private, user-controlled

Tap Log Trip to start. Tap to stop. The app records your route, direction, start and end mile, and duration. Trip data never leaves your device — MileCheck does not collect, transmit, or store your location history. Used by DOT road crews, snowplow drivers, and salt/sand/brine applicators to log their passes.

Who uses it on Android

Truckers built this. Everyone else benefits.

01

Long-haul and regional truckers

When something goes wrong on the highway — a breakdown, an accident, a medical situation — the first question dispatch asks is "where are you?" On rural interstates, there's no address. There's a mile marker. MileCheck gives it to you in a second, so you can give it to someone who can help.

02

DOT road crews and snowplow operators

Shift supervisors need to know which miles got treated and when. MileCheck's trip logging records the route, direction, and mile range automatically as you drive. Product selector (sand, salt, brine) is built in for DOT-specific documentation. No paper clipboard.

03

EMTs and first responders

Crash coordinates on a map aren't always actionable. "I-5 southbound at MP 174" is. MileCheck shows the nearest milepost so first responders can confirm or communicate exact incident location, and so incoming units know what exit to target.

04

Road trippers and commuters

See closures and crashes before you reach them — 10, 25, 50, or 100 miles out. The interactive map shows every alert on every route, at every zoom level. Filter to what matters. The mile marker display also works as a precision GPS speedometer.

Frequently asked

Questions about MileCheck on Android.

Is MileCheck available on Android?

Yes. MileCheck is available on Android via the Google Play Store. Search "MileCheck" or use the download link below. Both the Android and iOS versions have the same feature set — all 50 states, live alerts in 31 states, offline mode, trip logging, and interactive map.

Does it work on older Android phones?

MileCheck runs on Android 7.0 (Nougat) and above. It's designed to work well on budget and mid-range Android devices, not just flagships. The bundled milepost data and the GPS calculation engine are optimized to be lightweight.

Does it work without cell service?

Yes — mile marker display works completely offline. The milepost dataset for all 50 states is bundled inside the app when you download it. Live DOT road alerts require a cell or Wi-Fi connection to update, but the core mile marker feature does not.

How accurate is the mile marker on Android?

MileCheck shows your position interpolated between physical highway signs, to one decimal place. If you're between the MP 47 sign and the MP 48 sign, you see something like 47.3 — not just 47. GPS position is smoothed over three readings to reduce jitter. Accuracy depends on the underlying DOT milepost data for each state.

What's the difference between mile markers and exit numbers?

Mile markers are the small green signs that appear roughly every mile along interstate and US highways. They count up from the state's southern or western border. Exit numbers on most modern interstates match the nearest mile marker. They're not the same sign — and they're not always the same number. MileCheck shows mile markers, not exit numbers.

Which states have live road alerts?

Live DOT road alerts are currently active in 31 states: Alaska, Arizona, Arkansas, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, Florida, Georgia, Idaho, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Louisiana, Maine, Massachusetts, Minnesota, Montana, Nebraska, Nevada, New Hampshire, New York, North Carolina, Ohio, Oregon, South Carolina, Texas, Utah, Vermont, Washington, and Wisconsin. Mile markers work in all 50 states. More alert states are added regularly.

Is MileCheck free on Android?

The mile marker display — the core feature — is always free. Premium features (live road alerts in 31 states, interactive map, trip logging) cost $9.99 per year or $1.99 per month. There's a 7-day free trial so you can test everything before paying. There are no ads in either tier.

Does MileCheck track or store my location?

No. MileCheck uses your GPS location to calculate the nearest mile marker — that's it. Your location is never transmitted to MileCheck's servers. Trip logs are stored on your device only and can be deleted at any time. MileCheck does not collect, sell, or share location data.

Is MileCheck useful for truckers specifically?

Yes — truckers are one of MileCheck's primary audiences. When a truck breaks down or needs roadside assistance, the first thing dispatch or emergency services needs is an exact location. On rural interstates, that means a mile marker. MileCheck gives you that number instantly. It also shows DOT alerts ahead on your route and supports trip logging with start/end mile and optional notes.
