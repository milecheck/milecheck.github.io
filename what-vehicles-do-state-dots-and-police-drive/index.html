What vehicles do state DOTs and police drive?

Your mile marker on screen as you drive. Plus live alerts and cameras in the MileCheck app.App StoreGoogle Play

State, county and city governments own about 4.2 million vehicles, by their 2024 reports to the Federal Highway Administration. No national count says which makes they are. Seven states publish lists that do. In most of them the state police drive Dodge Chargers or Ford police vehicles, and DOT crews drive pickups and heavy trucks.

How many vehicles do state and local governments own?

4,215,404 motor vehicles in 2024. That is 1,295,922 cars, 462,306 buses and 2,457,176 trucks, owned by states, counties and cities together. Federal agencies had another 492,199 on the road in the states.

Connecticut, Michigan and Missouri reported none, so the real total is higher. FHWA publishes what each state reports. It does not break the count out by agency or by make.

What do state police and DOTs drive in each state?

Where a state publishes a list of its own vehicles with makes or models, the table shows the most common one for the state police and for the DOT. The Dodge Charger leads in California, Arkansas and Utah. A Ford police Explorer or police SUV leads in Illinois and Maine. Indiana's State Police units are mostly Dodge Durangos.

DOT crews mostly drive pickups and big trucks. The Ford F-150 leads in Illinois and Indiana, and the Chevrolet Silverado in California. Maine's and Utah's DOTs list more of one heavy truck than anything else, the International 7400 and the Mack GU713.

Who is counted: the government vehicles column counts every car, bus and truck owned by the state, its counties and its cities, as each state reported it to FHWA for 2024. The other columns count only the state police and the state DOT, from the state's own list. 13 more states publish totals without makes, and 30 publish no list we could find.
