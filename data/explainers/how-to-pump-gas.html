To pump gas at a self-service station, park next to the pump, turn off your engine and pay. Pick the grade your car needs, put the nozzle in and fill. Stop when the nozzle clicks off.

How do you pump gas, step by step?

Step 1. Park and turn off your engine. Pull up so the pump is on the same side as your fuel door. Put the car in park and shut off the engine. Don't smoke while fueling.

Step 2. Pay. You can usually pay by card at the pump or go inside and prepay. If you pay inside, tell the cashier your pump number and how much you want. Then follow the pump's instructions.

Step 3. Pick your grade. Check your owner's manual for the octane your car needs. Regular is usually 87 octane. Premium is usually 91 to 94. Octane measures how well gas resists engine knock. Higher octane isn't automatically better for your car.

Step 4. Open the fuel door. Some cars have a release button or lever inside. On others you press the door. Take off the cap if your car has one and set it somewhere it won't fall.

Step 5. Get rid of static. Touch a metal surface away from the nozzle before you pick it up. Stay out of your car while gas is pumping. Getting back in can build up a static charge.

Step 6. Put the nozzle in. Read the fuel label on the pump first. Lift the nozzle and push the spout fully into the fuel opening.

Step 7. Fill. Squeeze the handle. Hold it, or use the latch if the nozzle has one. Stay next to the pump while it fills.

Step 8. Stop at the click. The nozzle shuts off on its own when the tank is full. Don't squeeze again to top off. The EPA says topping off can cause spills, release gas vapors and damage your car's vapor recovery system. Put the nozzle back on the pump, then put your gas cap back on and close the fuel door.

Step 9. Finish up. Follow the pump's prompts to end the sale. Take your receipt if you want one. Check that the fuel door is closed before you drive away.

Does a green pump handle always mean diesel?

No. Green handles are common for diesel in the U.S., but handle colors aren't standard. AAA says to read the fuel label instead of going by color.

What should you do if gas spills or catches fire?

Stop pumping and tell the station attendant. Keep cigarettes and anything else that could spark away from the spill.

If a fire starts, don't pull the nozzle out of the car. Move away and call 911. Oregon's required pump warning signs say the same.
