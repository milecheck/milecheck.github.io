A street and an avenue are both names for roads, and there is no single U.S. rule that separates them. A city may use the words to show direction, road layout, or a local naming tradition.

An avenue is not automatically wider, busier, or more important than a street. The name alone does not tell you how many lanes a road has or which direction it runs.

Does a street run east to west and an avenue north to south?

In some places, yes. But it is a local convention, not a nationwide rule. A visitor who assumes that every avenue runs north to south can easily get turned around in another city.

Sequim, Washington, is a clear example. Its city code says public roads running north to south must be called avenues. Roads running east to west must be called streets. The city also permits other names, including lane, place, and court, for smaller roads. Those rules apply within Sequim, not across Washington or the country.

What about Washington, D.C.?

Washington, D.C., uses a different pattern. Its code designates broad diagonal highways as avenues named for states and territories. North-south streets are numbered, while east-west streets follow a letter-based naming system.

These examples show why the same road suffix can mean different things in different places. The local street plan and naming code matter more than a general definition.

Do all cities assign street types by direction?

No. Jacksonville, Florida, lists both street and avenue among its recognized road types, but says it has no general conventions for assigning most street types. It specifies some exceptions, including circles and courts.

Names also remain part of a city's addressing system. Consistent naming helps people find buildings and helps emergency crews locate an address. Sequim's code identifies emergency response as one reason for its system.

How can you tell which road you are on?

Use the full posted name, including Street or Avenue. Check the city or local map instead of guessing from the suffix. If two roads share a number, such as 4th Street and 4th Avenue, the last word matters when entering an address.

On longer drives, street names may give way to numbered highways. In that case, the highway designation is more useful for identifying your route.
