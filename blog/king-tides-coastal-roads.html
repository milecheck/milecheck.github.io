When are the next king tides on the West Coast, and which roads flood?

November 26, 2026 6 min read

The next West Coast king tides come around the full moons of December 23, 2026 and January 22, 2027. Oceanographer Greg Dusek told The Weather Channel that more king tide flooding is likely during Christmas week and again in late January. Floodwater rises and falls with the tide. A road that is dry at lunch can be closed a few hours later.

The November 24 to 26, 2026 king tides set records and closed stretches of US 101. This article covers which roads flooded and why. It also explains what to do when driving along the coast. The road details reflect conditions recorded on those dates, not current conditions.

Which roads flooded during the November 2026 king tides?

Oregon. On Tuesday, November 24, the tide gauge at Charleston, near Coos Bay, appeared to break its all-time record, set on New Year's Day 2006, according to the National Weather Service in Medford. US 101 near Coos Bay was shut down that day. OPB reported US 101 closed at H Street in Nehalem, part of Highway 34 in Waldport covered by water from Eckman Lake, and Catching Slough Road underwater near milepost 4, where several cars were stranded. All of those drivers got out.

Washington. US 101 near Raymond, on Willapa Bay, closed on Tuesday. The tide gauge at Westport hit 7 feet, major flood stage, on both Monday and Tuesday, and Marine View Drive in Ocean Shores flooded.

California. Six tide gauges tied or set all-time records on Wednesday, including Monterey, Santa Barbara and San Diego. In Marin County, Caltrans said the ramp from northbound US 101 to northbound State Route 1 in Mill Valley was flooding for four or five hours each day. Roads near San Francisco's Embarcadero and in Sausalito and Mill Valley were swamped, and Waymo paused its Bay Area robotaxis from Monday to Wednesday while several sat stranded on flooded San Francisco streets.

Sources: The Weather Channel, Nov. 26, 2026 · OPB, Nov. 25, 2026 · ABC7 San Francisco live updates

What a king tide actually is

"King tide" isn't a scientific term. NOAA describes it as a popular name for exceptionally high tides. They usually happen when two things line up: a new or full moon, when the sun and moon pull in the same direction, and the moon at perigee, its closest point to Earth. Oceanographers call that a perigean spring tide, and NOAA says it happens often between 6 and 8 times a year.

The extra height can be more than a foot over a normal spring tide. On a calm day that's a soggy parking lot. Add a storm surge, big waves and a warm ocean and it's a closed highway.

Sources: NOAA, What is a king tide? · NOAA, What is a perigean spring tide?

Why were the November 2026 king tides worse than usual?

That week stacked everything. The full moon on November 24 was a supermoon, and the moon reached its closest point to Earth the next day. The Weather Channel pinned the record flooding on king tides with a full moon, Pacific storms and El Niño-warmed ocean water. Forecasters warned of waves up to 25 to 30 feet along parts of the Oregon and California coast.

NOAA also notes a longer trend: as sea level rises, the minor flooding that comes with these tides is expected to happen more often.

Sources: The Weather Channel, Nov. 26, 2026 · Farmers' Almanac · NOAA, What is a king tide?

Why coastal roads get hit first

Many coastal roads, including long stretches of US 101, run right along bays, sloughs and estuaries, close to the waterline. When the tide is high enough, the low spots go under first. That's why the flooding is so tied to the clock. It peaks around high tide, then drains, then comes back with the next one.

If your drive follows the coast

  1. Check closures before you leave. Oregon officials pointed drivers to TripCheck. Each coastal state publishes its closures, and MileCheck pulls those official DOT feeds into one map with mile markers.
  2. Look at the tide times. If a low stretch floods at high tide, driving it a couple of hours earlier or later can be the difference between a normal trip and a detour.
  3. Never drive through standing water or around a barricade. Water hides the edge of the road and anything under it, and it can stall a car.
  4. Stay off jetties, seawalls and beaches. The Army Corps of Engineers warned people to stay off jetties, breakwaters, piers and seawalls, and sneaker waves are a real risk during king tides.
  5. Know where you are. If you do get stuck, tell 911 your highway, direction and nearest mile marker.

Sources: OPB, Nov. 25, 2026 · The Weather Channel, Nov. 26, 2026

When are the next king tides?

Oceanographer Greg Dusek told The Weather Channel that more king tide flooding is likely along the West Coast during Christmas week and again in late January. A San Mateo County supervisor called the November flooding in Pacifica a dress rehearsal for December and January.

The next full moons are supermoons too: December 23, 2026, the closest full moon of the year, and January 22, 2027. If you have holiday plans on the coast, those are the weeks to watch.

Sources: The Weather Channel, Nov. 26, 2026 · ABC7 San Francisco live updates · Farmers' Almanac
