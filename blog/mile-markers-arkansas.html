Highway Mile Markers in Arkansas

July 4, 2026 4 min read

Arkansas sits on one of the busiest truck corridors in America. I-40 carries a river of freight across the state every day, and the whole system — dispatchers, troopers, tow trucks, road crews — talks in mile markers. Here's how Arkansas mileposts work, where they matter most, and how to see yours plus live DOT alerts in real time.

How Arkansas numbers its highways

Arkansas mileposts count up from where a route enters the state and rise as you head north or east. On I-40, mile 0 sits at the Oklahoma line near Fort Smith and the numbers climb to roughly 285 at the Mississippi River across from Memphis. US highways and Arkansas state routes carry their own milepost lines. Mile marker data for every Arkansas highway is bundled into MileCheck and works offline.

The corridors people actually drive

  • I-40 crosses the state Fort Smith to West Memphis through Little Rock — about 285 miles of near-constant truck traffic.
  • I-30 runs the Texas corner up through Texarkana and Hot Springs country to Little Rock.
  • I-49 climbs the Ozark grades through Fayetteville and the fast-growing northwest corner.
  • I-55 hugs the Mississippi River in the east; US-65 and US-67 feed the corners of the state.

Where mile markers matter most: I-40 and the Ozarks

When something goes wrong on I-40 — a jackknifed truck, a work-zone backup, a river-bridge closure — the location comes over the radio as a mile marker, and traffic can stack up for miles behind it. Knowing your milepost tells you whether the incident is ahead of you or behind you. Up in the Ozarks, I-49's long grades and curves are the other spot where an exact location matters: fewer exits, fewer landmarks, and weather that changes with the elevation.

Flash floods and ice storms

Arkansas weather hits its highways from two directions. Spring and summer bring flash flooding — creeks that jump their banks and put water over the road with little warning, especially in the river valleys and the Ozark and Ouachita highlands. Winter brings ice storms that glaze the higher terrain while the lowlands stay wet. Both kinds of trouble get announced by mile marker, and both are exactly when a live alert before you commit to a stretch of highway pays off.

ARDOT publishes its traffic cameras on IDrive Arkansas. MileCheck's Arkansas cameras map shows them by route, from I-40 through Little Rock and the Delta to I-49 in the northwest, so a flooded stretch or an ice storm can be looked at before you reach it.

MileCheck pulls live Arkansas road alerts — closures, crashes, construction, and winter conditions — tied to the mile marker and direction, so you can see what's ahead of you on your route instead of finding out at the back of the queue.

Everyday and emergency

Whether you're running freight across I-40, reporting a breakdown on an Ozark grade, or telling a tow truck where to find you, the mile marker is the location Arkansas roads run on.
