Highway Mile Markers in Utah

July 8, 2026 4 min read

Utah packs everything into one state: a big-city corridor down I-15, a 7,000-foot winter summit twenty minutes from downtown Salt Lake, and the emptiest stretch of interstate in America. All of it runs on mile markers. Here's how they work across Utah, where they matter most, and how to see your exact one plus live UDOT conditions in real time.

How Utah numbers its highways

Utah's mileposts count up from where a route enters the state on its south or west side — I-15 starts at mile 0 at the Arizona line below St. George and runs about 400 miles to Idaho. I-80 counts up from the Nevada line at Wendover; I-70 counts up from its western start at Cove Fort. UDOT announces everything — closures, crashes, plow status, canyon restrictions — by milepost. Mile marker data for every Utah highway is bundled into the app and works offline.

The corridors people actually drive

  • I-15 is Utah's spine — St. George, Cedar City, Provo, Salt Lake, Ogden, about 400 miles border to border.
  • I-80 comes in from Nevada across the salt flats, through Salt Lake, up Parley's Canyon past Park City, and out to Wyoming.
  • I-70 starts at Cove Fort and crosses the San Rafael Swell to Colorado — including the famous empty stretch.
  • US-89 threads the towns the interstates skip; US-191 drops to Moab and the parks; US-6 connects Provo to Green River through Price.

Where mile markers matter most: the empty quarter

I-70 between Salina and Green River is the longest stretch of interstate in America with no services — about 106 miles, roughly mileposts 56 to 162, with no gas, no food, and long gaps with no cell signal. Out there the mileposts aren't trivia; they're the only continuous reference that exists. On the other side of the state, Parley's Summit on I-80 — about 7,000 feet, minutes from Salt Lake — turns into a chain-law winter road fast enough to catch commuters in dress shoes.

Canyons, chains, and lake-effect snow

Utah winters are canyon stories. Parley's Canyon on I-80 sees chain laws and closures all season. Sardine Canyon on US-89/91 and the Cottonwood canyons above Salt Lake close for storms and avalanche control — and UDOT's restrictions come and go by the hour. Lake-effect snow off the Great Salt Lake can bury one stretch of I-15 while ten miles away is dry. Every restriction and closure is announced by milepost, and getting the live UDOT alert before you're in the canyon is the difference between rerouting and waiting it out.

UDOT also publishes where its plows are. MileCheck shows them on the Utah cameras map and on the Parleys Summit page, each truck with the route and mile marker it is on and how long ago it reported. A truck on the map is a position, not proof the road is plowed. The Parleys page also carries the snow depth at the Parleys Summit station and the Utah Avalanche Center's rating for the Salt Lake zone.

Live UDOT alerts, in one place

MileCheck pulls live road alerts straight from UDOT: closures, crashes, construction, mountain-pass conditions, and roadside weather stations, tied to the mile marker and direction so you can see what's ahead of you on your route — the canyon status before the canyon, the empty stretch before the last gas station.

Everyday and emergency

Whether you're crossing the Swell on I-70 where the next town is an hour out, timing Parley's on a storm day, or reporting a breakdown south of Moab, the mile marker is the location Utah's roads run on. MileCheck shows yours in real time, down to a tenth of a mile — offline included, which is exactly what the empty quarter demands.
