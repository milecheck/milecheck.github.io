Labor Day Weekend 2026

Here is what we know for Labor Day weekend

September 4, 2026

We checked every state DOT feed and British Columbia for the road work and closures already on the books for Labor Day weekend, September 4–7, 2026. Counts are a snapshot taken 12:34 PM Pacific on September 4 — live totals move, so treat these as a starting point and check your state before you go.

The national picture

AAA's booking data points to strong domestic travel demand over Labor Day weekend. Hertz expects Friday, September 4 to be the busiest rental-car pickup day. (AAA)

Worst windows left to drive per AAA/INRIX
  • Friday, Sept 4: noon–8 PM
  • Saturday, Sept 5: 1–5 PM
  • Monday, Sept 7 (Labor Day): 2–5 PM
  • Sunday, Sept 6 didn’t surface a peak window — it looks like the calmest day to drive
Sample high-delay routes in AAA/INRIX's 2026 forecast
  • Seattle to Ellensburg, I-90 East — Monday 7:15 PM, travel time up 121%
  • Boston to Providence, I-95 South — Friday 3:30 PM, travel time up 105%
  • San Francisco to Sacramento, I-80 East — Friday 5:45 PM, travel time up 81%
  • Indianapolis to Chicago, I-65 North — Friday 5:15 PM, travel time up 76%
  • Los Angeles to San Diego, I-5 South — Friday 6:00 PM, travel time up 75%

Those are metro-area peak-congestion estimates from AAA's 2026 Labor Day release, not a ranked national list — treat them as examples of how bad a normal drive can get at the worst hour. (AAA)

See these corridors on MileCheck's live maps: I-5, I-10, I-80, I-90, I-95, or browse all corridors. For any other state, the full interactive map is at milecheckapp.com/maps.

Labor Day also closes out the “100 Deadliest Days,” the Memorial Day–to–Labor Day stretch when traffic fatalities historically peak. Several states are running extra DUI enforcement through the holiday: Colorado's “The Heat is On” crackdown runs September 3–9 (CDOT), and Utah's statewide DUI patrols, part of the national “Drive Sober or Get Pulled Over” campaign, run through September 7 (Gephardt Daily). If you're driving through the evening or the holiday itself, that's where to be extra careful.

How much roadwork is standing on your route

We pulled every state DOT feed plus British Columbia and counted the roadwork and closure records whose posted dates overlap September 4–7. A few things are worth knowing about that number before you read it.

Most of it isn't holiday work. It's long-running projects that happen to still be standing when you drive through — so for every state we split out how many actually start or end during the weekend itself. Nationally, of 5,449 records only 2,274 do.

Ramp and exit closures are left out. They're real, but they rarely change which road you take.

These are scheduled closures, not live traffic. Everything below was posted in advance by a state DOT. A lane that closes this morning, a crash at 2 PM, a backup that builds in twenty minutes — none of that is in here. That is what your state's live map, and our app, are for.

Only 24 of the 51 state/province feeds carry usable dates. The rest can't be counted at all — for those we say so plainly and link you straight to the DOT instead of guessing. Two feeds failed us when we ran this — one didn’t respond, one returned nothing usable — and we say that rather than reporting an empty road.

Crossing into Canada or the US

Peace Arch is a busy passenger crossing between Surrey, BC and Blaine, Washington. Check live waits before leaving. Depending on your route and each port's hours, Pacific Highway, Lynden, or Sumas may be alternatives. (WSDOT)

At crossings with Ready Lanes, travelers with eligible RFID-enabled passport cards or Enhanced Driver's Licenses may use those lanes. NEXUS members may use dedicated NEXUS lanes where available. Check lane availability and make sure every traveler has an eligible document. (CBP) CBP's live wait times for every port are at bwt.cbp.gov, and MileCheck tracks 85 border crossings — 30 on the Canadian border, 55 on the Mexican.
