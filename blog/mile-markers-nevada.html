Highway Mile Markers in Nevada

July 4, 2026 4 min read

Nevada is the most mile-marker-dependent state in the country. Between the towns there is basin, range, and not much else — which means the milepost on the shoulder is often the only address a stretch of road has. Here's how Nevada's markers work, and how to see your exact one plus live conditions in real time.

How Nevada numbers its highways

Nevada's mileposts count up as routes cross the state. On I-80 that means mile marker 0 at the California line near Verdi, with the numbers climbing east through Reno, Winnemucca, and Elko to the Utah line near Wendover, around mile 410. NDOT signs its routes as I, US, and SR. Mile marker data for every Nevada highway is bundled into MileCheck and works offline.

The corridors people actually drive

  • I-80 crosses roughly 410 miles of northern Nevada, from the California line through Reno, Winnemucca, and Elko to Wendover.
  • US-50 — "The Loneliest Road in America" — runs about 400 miles across the middle of the state, with towns an hour apart.
  • US-95 links Las Vegas and Reno the long way, through hundreds of miles of open desert.
  • I-15 carries the Las Vegas corridor between California and Arizona.

Where mile markers matter most: the Loneliest Road

US-50 earned its nickname honestly. Between Fallon, Austin, Eureka, and Ely, the towns sit about an hour apart, and in between there's no gas, no cross streets, and often no cell bars. If you break down out there, your location IS your mile marker — it's the reference a tow dispatcher or a 911 operator will ask for, and it's the one thing that pins you to a specific point on 400 miles of nearly identical two-lane. The same math applies to US-95, US-6, and most of rural Nevada.

Weather across the basins

Nevada's hazards flip with the seasons: summer heat that punishes a stranded vehicle, and winter storms that shut I-80 over the ranges and glaze US-50's summits with black ice. High-wind restrictions and closures come and go quickly on the open stretches, and NDOT announces every one of them by route and milepost. Getting that alert before you leave the last town with services matters more here than almost anywhere.

Everyday and emergency

Whether you're crossing US-50 in July, reporting a breakdown outside Winnemucca, or just telling a friend where to meet you, the mile marker is the location Nevada's roads run on.
