Labor Day Weekend Road Recap

by MileCheck · September 8, 2026 · revised September 22, 2026

What the state road feeds showed from Thursday, Sept. 3 through Labor Day, Monday, Sept. 7, 2026, in all 50 states and British Columbia. Crashes, closures, border waits, Seattle's drawbridges, wildfires, and the first ice of the season.

How we got these numbers: MileCheck saves every state department of transportation (DOT) road alert as it's published. We counted what was on those feeds from Sept. 3 through Sept. 7. Not all states report the same data. Fire figures are as of Labor Day, Sept. 7, 4:50 PM Pacific; border waits are from U.S. Customs and Border Protection, saved every hour, in each crossing's local time. Rhode Island and New Mexico returned no records for the five days, and North Carolina and Idaho crash reports were not in our feed.

Closures and roadwork

1,971 closures and 7,617 roadwork records were on the feeds over the five days, across all 51 jurisdictions. Roadwork did not stop for the holiday. The number of roadwork records on the feeds dipped 5% on Saturday and was back near Thursday's level by Monday.

Seven records named Labor Day, and all of them were parades. Keokuk, Iowa closed US-136 and US-218. Malden, Indiana closed IN-49. Hoisington, Kansas closed US-281 for its festival. Pioche, Nevada closed SR-321 and SR-322.

  • San Ysidro (California) — 200 min, Monday at 2 AM
  • Del Rio (Texas) — 180 min, Monday at 6 PM

  • Highgate Springs (Vermont) — 170 min, Monday at 4 PM

Blaine's Peace Arch crossing peaked at 50 minutes Sunday at 9 PM and averaged 9 minutes over the five days. On Labor Day the slowest hour there was 1 PM and the fastest was 5 PM. Live waits for all 85 crossings are at milecheckapp.com/borders.

Wildfires

Wildfires are still affecting western states and British Columbia. As of Labor Day, Washington had 48 active fires, including two over 160,000 acres that were not yet contained. Oregon's biggest fires were mostly contained by the weekend. Across every state and province we track, 483 active fires covered 5,835,642 acres.

Largest fires by state, as of Labor Day, Sept. 7, 4:50 PM Pacific
  • Oregon: 74 active fires, 2,889,707 acres. Big Grass (Malheur County), 578,637 acres, 94% contained; Rowe Creek Complex (Wheeler County), 373,927 acres, 99% contained; Crosswhite (Wheeler County), 342,923 acres, 92% contained; Coleman Creek (Harney County), 308,863 acres, 99% contained.
  • British Columbia: 70 active fires, 1,024,216 acres. Pear Lake, 409,970 acres, containment not published; Ainslie Creek, 167,346 acres, containment not published; French Bar Creek, 152,676 acres, containment not published.
  • Washington: 48 active fires, 652,207 acres. Little Giant (Chelan County), 171,163 acres, 81% contained; Sinlahekin (Okanogan County), 166,116 acres, 59% contained; Kaiser Canyon (Okanogan County), 138,293 acres, 100% contained; Modrite (Ferry County), 57,594 acres, 100% contained.
  • Idaho: 20 active fires, 208,599 acres. Tartar (Washington County), 158,027 acres, 100% contained; Crooked (Boise County), 10,118 acres, 60% contained; Cascade (Idaho County), 7,475 acres, 50% contained.
  • Utah: 12 active fires, 198,209 acres. Widemouth 2 (Millard County), 129,741 acres, 97% contained; Snyder (Grand County), 28,264 acres, 100% contained; Rocky Canyon (Morgan County), 16,464 acres, 100% contained.
  • Colorado: 10 active fires, 162,548 acres. Aspen Acres (Custer County), 102,004 acres, 82% contained; Gold Mountain (Ouray County), 39,747 acres, 92% contained; Willow (Lake County), 7,534 acres, 80% contained.
  • Nevada: 9 active fires, 154,788 acres. Mouse Meadow (Nye County), 52,000 acres, 100% contained; McConnell (Humboldt County), 33,241 acres, 83% contained; Calico (Humboldt County), 18,319 acres, 98% contained.
  • Montana: 31 active fires, 114,986 acres. Sand Creek (Beaverhead County), 35,509 acres, 36% contained; Rattlesnake Complex (Prairie County), 15,582 acres, 97% contained; Snow (Custer County), 14,056 acres, 97% contained.

Live fire perimeters and the closures near them are at milecheckapp.com/fire.

Weather

Monarch Pass, Colorado was icy on Labor Day weekend. US-50 at milepost 199 eastbound read icy with a road surface between 16°F and 18°F on all five days. I-70 at Silverthorne and near Dumont read icy on single days. Alaska's Dalton Highway reported snow on the roadway at the Sag River and at Chandalar. No other jurisdiction reported ice or snow on a road surface.
