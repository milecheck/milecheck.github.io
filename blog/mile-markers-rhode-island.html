Highway Mile Markers in Rhode Island

July 4, 2026 4 min read

Rhode Island is the smallest state in the country — I-95 crosses the whole thing in about 43 miles, and you can drive border to border in under an hour. The mile markers still matter, though, and here's how they work, plus where MileCheck's coverage stands.

How Rhode Island numbers its highways

Rhode Island's mileposts count up from the south and west. On I-95, mile 0 sits at the Connecticut line near Westerly, and the numbers climb about 43 miles northeast through Providence to the Massachusetts border — the shortest I-95 crossing of any state on the entire route. Mile marker data for every Rhode Island highway is bundled into MileCheck and works offline.

The corridors people actually drive

  • I-95 is the spine — the Connecticut line, Warwick, downtown Providence, then Massachusetts.
  • I-295 loops west of Providence as the bypass, rejoining I-95 on both ends.
  • US-1 runs the South County shore — the beach road to Narragansett, Charlestown, and Westerly.
  • RI-146 cuts northwest from Providence toward Worcester, and US-6 heads west into the hills.

Where mile markers matter most

Small state, real traffic. I-95 through Providence carries the full weight of the Northeast corridor, and a stall at rush hour backs up fast. In summer, the US-1 beach runs load up with shore traffic, and a breakdown with the family in the car is still a "what's your mile marker?" conversation with dispatch or the tow company. Being able to cross the state in 43 minutes doesn't help much when you're stopped on the shoulder at mile 12.

Coastal weather on a small canvas

Rhode Island's weather comes off the water — nor'easters, coastal flooding on the low shore roads, and winter ice that hits the whole state at once because there isn't much state to hit. When conditions turn, incident reports on I-95 and I-295 are still pinned to mile markers, and on US-1 a milepost is often the clearest way to describe a spot between beach towns.

Where Rhode Island alert coverage stands

Straight answer: mile markers for every Rhode Island highway are fully covered in MileCheck and work offline — but live RIDOT alerts aren't in the app yet, because RIDOT doesn't publish a public data feed for them. Most neighboring states do, and Connecticut and Massachusetts alerts are already live in MileCheck, so cross-border drives are covered on the other side of the line. The day RIDOT opens up a feed, we'll add it.

Everyday and emergency

Whether you're reporting a breakdown north of Providence, flagging a stall on the I-295 bypass, or telling a friend which US-1 beach exit you're near, the mile marker is the location Rhode Island's roads run on. MileCheck shows yours in real time, down to a tenth of a mile, with CarPlay and Android Auto — even where the signal dips.
