Highway Mile Markers in North Carolina

July 4, 2026 4 min read

Few states pack as much variety into one interstate as North Carolina: I-40 starts in mountain fog and ends at the beach. Here's how mile markers work across the state, where they matter most, and how to see your exact one plus live NCDOT conditions in real time.

How North Carolina numbers its highways

North Carolina's mileposts count up from where a route enters the state on the west or south and climb as you head east or north. On I-40, mile 0 sits at the Tennessee line in the Smokies and the numbers rise all the way to roughly 420 at Wilmington. State routes carry the NC prefix — NC-12 out on the Outer Banks is the famous one. Mile marker data for every North Carolina highway is bundled into MileCheck and works offline.

The corridors people actually drive

  • I-40 is the state's spine — about 420 miles from the mountains at the Tennessee line through Asheville, Winston-Salem, and Raleigh to the coast at Wilmington.
  • I-95 carries the whole East Coast through the flat farmland of the eastern part of the state.
  • I-85 and I-77 link Charlotte and the Piedmont to Virginia and South Carolina.
  • US-70 and NC-12 reach out toward the Outer Banks — the routes that matter most when a hurricane is coming.

Where mile markers matter most

West of Asheville, I-40 threads the Pigeon River Gorge — a curving mountain stretch known for rockslides and sudden closures, always announced by mile marker. On the other end of the state, hurricane evacuations for the Outer Banks move along US-70 and NC-12, and the orders are given by mile and route. And on I-95, one flat pine mile looks a lot like the next; the milepost is often the only landmark you have.

Hurricanes, rockslides, and mountain weather

North Carolina gets weather from both ends. On the coast, hurricane season means evacuation orders, lane changes, and closures on US-70, NC-12, and I-40 east of Raleigh — all communicated by mile marker. In the mountains, winter ice and rockslides can shut I-40 through the gorge for days at a time. Either way, the milepost is how NCDOT tells you where the problem is, and knowing yours is how you tell help where you are.

Everyday and emergency

Whether you're crossing the mountains in January, riding out a coastal evacuation, or reporting a breakdown on a featureless stretch of I-95, the mile marker is the location North Carolina's roads run on.
