Highway Mile Markers in Wisconsin

July 4, 2026 4 min read

Wisconsin's main road runs corner to corner — Milwaukee to Madison to Eau Claire to the Minnesota line — and in the middle of it, two interstates share one stretch of pavement. Here's how the mile markers work, where they get confusing, and how to see your exact one plus live 511WI conditions in real time.

How Wisconsin numbers its highways

Wisconsin's mileposts count up from where a route enters the state. On I-94, mile 0 sits at the Minnesota line near Hudson, and the numbers climb east and south — Eau Claire around mile 70, the Dells near 190, Madison around 240, Milwaukee in the 330s. Mile marker data for Wisconsin highways is bundled into MileCheck and works offline.

The corridors people actually drive

  • I-94 is the spine: MN line, Eau Claire, the Dells, Madison, Milwaukee, then south toward Chicago.
  • I-90 enters at La Crosse and joins I-94 through the Dells before splitting off toward Beloit and Illinois.
  • I-43 links Milwaukee and Green Bay along the lake side.
  • US-51 / I-39 runs up the middle toward Wausau; US-2 and US-53 serve the far north and Superior.

One road, two numbers: the Dells overlap

Through the Wisconsin Dells area, I-90 and I-94 run on the same pavement — one stretch of road, two route numbers, one milepost line. It's a classic confusion: your GPS says I-90, the overhead signs say I-90/94, and the person you're describing your location to has heard a third version. The mile marker cuts through all of it. Whatever the route number on the shield, the milepost is the same, and it's the number dispatch and tow drivers actually use.

Deer, lake-effect, and the long white winter

Wisconsin's hazard driving is seasonal and predictable in the worst way. Deer collisions spike at dawn and dusk in fall — a fact of life on every Wisconsin highway, and when one happens, the mile marker is what you give the dispatcher. In winter, lake-effect snow off Superior hits the far northwest around US-2 and US-53, and blowing snow shuts down open stretches statewide. Closures and pileup warnings are announced by route and milepost, so knowing yours — and what's ahead — matters most exactly when conditions are worst.

Everyday and emergency

Whether you're crossing the state on I-94, threading the Dells overlap, or reporting a deer strike on a county-dark stretch at dusk, the mile marker is the location Wisconsin's roads run on.
