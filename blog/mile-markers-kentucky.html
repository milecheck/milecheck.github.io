Highway Mile Markers in Kentucky

July 4, 2026 4 min read

Kentucky has a navigation trap most states don't: highways whose names differ by a single letter but run entirely different routes. US-31E and US-31W. US-25E and US-25W. Get the letter wrong and your mile marker points to the wrong road. Here's how Kentucky's mileposts work, where the traps are, and how to see your exact location plus live road alerts in real time.

How Kentucky numbers its highways

Kentucky's mileposts count up from where a route enters the state and rise as you head north or east. On I-75, mile 0 sits at the Tennessee line and the numbers climb to roughly 192 at the Ohio River across from Cincinnati. US highways and Kentucky state routes carry their own milepost lines — and so do the bourbon-country parkways, which go by their own names entirely. The corridors people actually drive

  • I-75 runs the eastern spine of the state, Tennessee line through London and Lexington to Cincinnati.
  • I-64 crosses west to east through Louisville, Frankfort, and Lexington.
  • I-71 links Louisville to Cincinnati; the three interstates converge on Louisville and Lexington.
  • The parkways — Kentucky's bourbon-country expressways carry their own names and their own milepost lines.

The directional-suffix trap: 31E vs 31W

In most states, "E" or "W" after a route number means a direction of travel. In Kentucky, it can mean a different highway. US-31E and US-31W split and run separate routes through the state; US-25E and US-25W do the same in the southeast. Each carries its own milepost line, so "US-31, mile 40" is ambiguous in a way that can send help to the wrong road. Always give — and always check — the full route name, letter included. MileCheck shows the exact route you're on along with the milepost, so the ambiguity never leaves your phone.

Fog, ice, and the hill country

Eastern Kentucky's hill country brings its own driving weather — dense valley fog in the mornings, ice on the grades in winter, and flash flooding in the hollows after heavy rain. When a slide or flood closes a road, the closure is announced by route and milepost, and on the rural routes and parkways that milepost is often the only reference around.
