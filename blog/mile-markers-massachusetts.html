Highway Mile Markers in Massachusetts

July 4, 2026 4 min read

Massachusetts made mile markers matter more than almost any other state — in 2020 it renumbered its highway exits so the exit number is the mile marker. Here's how the system works, from the Berkshires to Boston, and how to see your exact milepost plus live MassDOT alerts in real time.

How Massachusetts numbers its highways

Massachusetts mileposts count up from the west and south. On I-90 — the Massachusetts Turnpike, the MassPike — mile 0 sits at the New York line and the numbers climb roughly 135 miles east to Boston. The Pike is a toll road with its own milepost tradition, and it's the state's clearest example of the big change: in 2020, MassDOT converted exit numbers statewide from sequential to milepost-based. Exit 90 now sits near mile 90. Know your exit and you know your mile; know your mile and you know your next exit. Mile marker data for every Massachusetts highway is bundled into MileCheck and works offline.

The corridors people actually drive

  • I-90 (MassPike) is the east–west spine — the New York line, the Berkshires, Springfield, Worcester, Boston.
  • I-95 arcs around Boston as the beltway's outer ring and runs the coast toward Rhode Island and New Hampshire.
  • I-495 is the outer orbit through the commuter belt; I-93 punches north–south through downtown Boston.
  • US-6 carries the Cape traffic, and Route 2 runs the Mohawk Trail across the north of the state.

Where mile markers matter most

The Pike through the Berkshires is the loneliest stretch — long gaps between exits, real winter weather in the hills, and a breakdown at night is a milepost conversation with dispatch. Around Boston it's the opposite problem: so many overlapping routes and ramps that "on 95 near the split" means five different places. A route plus a mile marker is the one description that's unambiguous in both worlds — and since the exit renumbering, it's also how every Massachusetts exit sign already talks to you.

Nor'easters and the winter Pike

Massachusetts winters bring nor'easters that can shut down travel statewide, and the Pike through the Berkshire hills catches snow and ice earlier and harder than the coast. Speed restrictions, closures, and crash reports during storms are given by route and milepost, so a live alert tied to a mile number tells you exactly whether the trouble is ahead of you or behind you.
