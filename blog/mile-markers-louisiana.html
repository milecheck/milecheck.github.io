Highway Mile Markers in Louisiana

July 4, 2026 4 min read

Louisiana is bridge country. Its interstates spend a remarkable share of their miles up on pilings over water and swamp, and out there the mile marker isn't just handy — it's the only address the road has. Here's how Louisiana's mileposts work, where they matter most, and how to see yours plus live DOT alerts in real time.

How Louisiana numbers its highways

Louisiana's mileposts count up from where a route enters the state and rise as you head north or east. On I-10, mile 0 sits at the Texas line west of Lake Charles, and the numbers climb all the way past mile 235 around New Orleans. US highways and Louisiana state routes carry their own milepost lines. Mile marker data for every Louisiana highway is bundled into MileCheck and works offline.

The corridors people actually drive

  • I-10 runs the width of the state, Texas line through Lake Charles, Lafayette, Baton Rouge, and New Orleans.
  • I-20 crosses north Louisiana through Shreveport and Monroe.
  • I-49 links Lafayette north through Alexandria to Shreveport.
  • I-12 is the Baton Rouge–Slidell shortcut north of Lake Pontchartrain; US-90 traces the bayou country along the coast.

Where mile markers matter most: the Atchafalaya Basin Bridge

Between Baton Rouge and Lafayette, I-10 crosses the Atchafalaya swamp on roughly 18 miles of continuous elevated causeway. There are no exits, no landmarks, and swamp on both sides — if you break down or witness a crash out there, the mile marker is the only location reference you have. It's the clearest example anywhere of why knowing your milepost matters. The same goes for the long spans over Lake Pontchartrain and the bridges threading the bayous on US-90.

Hurricanes, fog, and contraflow

When a hurricane approaches the coast, Louisiana runs contraflow on I-10 — reversing lanes to move everyone inland — and the whole operation is coordinated by milepost: where contraflow begins, where crossovers sit, where it ends. Outside of storm season, dense morning fog on the basin bridges and sudden thunderstorm flooding are the everyday hazards, and both get reported by mile marker too.

Everyday and emergency

Whether you're 9 miles out on the basin bridge with a flat, reporting a crash on I-20, or just telling a tow truck where to find you, the mile marker is the location Louisiana's roads run on.
