Highway Mile Markers in Idaho

July 4, 2026 4 min read

From the Snake River plain to the panhandle passes, Idaho's highways run on mile markers — and out on the long empty stretches, the milepost is often the only address there is. Here's how they work across the state, and how to see your exact one plus live conditions in real time.

How Idaho numbers its highways

Idaho's mileposts rise from south to north and from west to east. On I-84 that means mile marker 0 at the Oregon line, with the numbers climbing east past Boise and Twin Falls toward the Utah line around mile 275. State highways are signed SH or ID. Mile marker data for every Idaho highway is bundled into MileCheck and works offline.

The corridors people actually drive

  • I-84 crosses southern Idaho — roughly 275 miles from the Oregon line through Boise and past Twin Falls toward Utah.
  • I-90 clips the panhandle, climbing over Fourth of July Pass and Lookout Pass to the Montana line near mile 74.
  • US-95 runs the entire state north to south, roughly 538 miles from Nevada to Canada.
  • I-15 carries eastern Idaho traffic through Pocatello and Idaho Falls; US-20 heads toward Yellowstone.

Where mile markers matter most: the long gaps

Idaho's defining road condition is distance. US-95 between towns, US-20 across the high desert, ID-75 up the Sawtooth Valley — these are stretches where the next services can be an hour away and the cell signal comes and goes. When something goes wrong out there, "I'm near milepost 138 on US-95" is the sentence that gets help to you. There's no cross street to name and often no landmark a dispatcher would recognize.

Winter in the panhandle

Up north, I-90 climbs over Fourth of July Pass east of Coeur d'Alene and Lookout Pass at the Montana line near milepost 74 — both see heavy snow, and both get chain restrictions and closures announced by mile marker. Southern Idaho isn't spared either: ground blizzards and black ice shut sections of I-84 and I-86 across the Snake River plain most winters, and the closure boundaries are given as mileposts.

Everyday and emergency

Whether you're checking Lookout Pass in January, reporting a breakdown on US-95, or just telling a friend where to meet you, the mile marker is the location Idaho's roads run on. MileCheck shows yours in real time, down to a tenth of a mile, even where the cell signal drops.
