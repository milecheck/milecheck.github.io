Highway Mile Markers in Delaware

July 4, 2026 4 min read

Delaware is the second-smallest state, but its highways punch far above their size — the whole Northeast corridor squeezes through it. Here's how mile markers work in the First State, and how to see your exact one plus live DelDOT alerts in real time.

How Delaware numbers its highways

Delaware mileposts count up from the southern or western end of each route. On I-95, mile 0 sits at the Maryland line and the Pennsylvania line arrives around mile 23. Because the numbers are small, precision matters more here than almost anywhere — the difference between mile 5 and mile 6 near Wilmington can be the difference between two different interchanges. MileCheck shows your position down to a tenth of a mile, with every Delaware highway bundled to work offline.

The corridors people actually drive

  • I-95 crosses just about 23 miles of Delaware, but carries the entire East Coast through-traffic stream past Wilmington.
  • DE-1 is the state's own toll road, the main run south toward Dover and the beach towns.
  • US-13 is the old north–south spine down the length of the state.
  • I-295 and I-495 handle the Delaware Memorial Bridge approach and the Wilmington bypass.

Where mile markers matter most

Twenty-three miles of I-95 sounds easy until you're in it on a Friday: the toll plaza, the Wilmington curves, and the constant merge of I-295 and I-495 traffic make it one of the busiest short interstate stretches in the country. When something goes wrong there, or on the DE-1 beach run in summer, the location is given by mile marker — and small numbers mean there's no room to be a mile off.

Beach traffic, storms, and drawbridges

Delaware's seasonal story is the beach run: DE-1 south to Rehoboth and the coast fills up every summer weekend, and coastal storms can flood low-lying shore routes. Delaware is also drawbridge country along its canals and inlets — a movable span opening for boat traffic can stop a beach-bound line cold. Everyday and emergency

Whether you're threading I-95 at rush hour, heading down DE-1 on a summer Saturday, or reporting a breakdown to roadside assistance, the mile marker is the fastest way to say exactly where you are.
