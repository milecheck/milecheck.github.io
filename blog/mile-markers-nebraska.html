Highway Mile Markers in Nebraska

July 4, 2026 4 min read

Nebraska is 455 miles of I-80 along the Platte River — one of the longest, straightest drives in America — plus sandhills two-lanes that stay empty for hours. Out here, the mile marker is the address. Here's how it all works, plus live Nebraska DOT conditions in real time.

How Nebraska numbers its highways

Nebraska's mile markers count up from the west and the south. On I-80, mile 0 sits at the Wyoming line and the numbers climb east to Omaha on the Missouri River. When the Nebraska DOT closes a road for weather or roadwork, the announcement comes as a route and a mile range. Mile marker data for every Nebraska highway is bundled into MileCheck and works offline — which matters in the sandhills, where the cell signal quits long before the road does.

The corridors people actually drive

  • I-80 runs about 455 miles from the Wyoming line through North Platte, Kearney, and Lincoln to Omaha — the Platte River road, including a famously straight stretch of roughly 70 miles.
  • I-76 splits off toward Denver near the Colorado corner; I-29 brushes the eastern edge at Omaha.
  • US-83 runs north–south through North Platte; US-30, the original Lincoln Highway, shadows I-80 up the valley.
  • NE-2 crosses the sandhills from Grand Island toward Alliance — hours of open range and one of the loneliest, prettiest drives in the country.

Where mile markers matter most: the long straight

On a road where the horizon doesn't change for an hour, "where are you?" has exactly one useful answer: the milepost. Truckers running I-80 through the night live by the mile markers — for fuel stops, for weigh stations, for telling dispatch where the load is. And on the sandhills two-lanes, where you can drive a long time between towns, the milepost is what you give the tow truck.

Winter gates I-80 by the mile

Nebraska winters close I-80 more often than most drivers expect. Blizzards and ice roll down out of Wyoming and the DOT gates the interstate by mile range — western Nebraska can shut while Lincoln stays dry. The closure is posted as mileposts, the reopening is posted as mileposts, and your own milepost is what tells you which side of the gate you're on and which exit town has the last open motel.

Everyday and emergency

Whether you're running the full 455 in a day, crossing the sandhills on NE-2, or stopped on the shoulder with the wind rocking the car, the mile marker is the location Nebraska's roads run on.
