The mismatch

A GPS coordinate doesn't fail. It just doesn't match anything.

A telematics platform records a truck's position the way its GPS chip reports it — latitude and longitude. State DOTs, 911 dispatch, and roadside assistance all work the other way, by route and mile marker, because that's how mile-marker signs, DOT incident logs, and highway patrol reports are all filed. A dispatcher who wants to know whether a stretch is closed, under construction, or under a chain restriction has to convert the coordinate back to a route and mile marker first, then go look that up somewhere else — usually while something is already going wrong.

Virginia DOT says it plainly in its own driver guidance: “When calling #77 or 911 for emergency assistance, providing the information on a mile marker sign will help responders reach you quickly.” MileCheck's road-conditions layer is built from that same direction — every alert already tied to a route and mile marker, because that's how the state DOT that issued it filed it. Hand it a vehicle's coordinate and it runs the same lookup the phone app runs: nearest mile marker, on this route, in this direction, against live DOT data.
