What is in each row

One row is one mile marker. The route is the one a driver reads on the sign, like I-15 or US-89, not an internal agency code.

FieldWhat it holds
idA stable row id. It survives coordinate corrections, so you can match rows from one month to the next.
state, countryTwo-letter state code, and US.
routeThe signed route, for example I-15, US-89 or SR-201.
route_altOther signed routes on the same pavement at that marker.
agency_route_idThe agency's own route name, where it differs. Oregon highway numbers, for example.
marker, unitThe number on the marker, whole or one decimal, and mi.
directionNB, SB, EB or WB where the state places a marker on each side of the road.
lat, lonWGS84, six decimal places.
countyWhen the state supplies it.
position_typeWhether the point is a surveyed sign or placed along the state's route line at that mile.
source, source_url, licenseThe agency layer the row came from and its stated terms.

Coverage

  • All 50 states, interstates, US routes and state routes where the state publishes markers.
  • 24 states give surveyed sign positions. 19 are placed along the state's own route line at each mile. 7 use surveyed signs where they exist and the route line elsewhere. Every row says which.
  • Each state's source agency and its stated terms are listed in the manifest and in LICENSE.txt.
  • Kilometre markers for ten Canadian provinces and territories are available on request, in the same layout.

How you get it

  • Delivered to your S3 bucket, or by a secure download link.
  • One file per state and one national file, in CSV and GeoJSON.
  • A full snapshot on the 20th of every month, with a change list naming the markers added, moved and removed since the last one.
  • If a state changes its markers mid-month, you get the update without waiting for the next snapshot.
  • A manifest with row counts per state and SHA-256 checksums for every file.

We're based in Seattle and we know the roads. MileCheck started as a phone app, and drivers, including DOT crews, have sent us feedback on it for months. Data privacy

The Milepost Dataset comes as CSV and GeoJSON files that run on your own systems. The files contain no code that reports back to MileCheck. After delivery, we receive nothing about how your apps or vehicles use them. Our delivery service may log basic download details, such as the time and network address. If you use the MileCheck API instead, different handling applies, and our privacy policy describes it.
