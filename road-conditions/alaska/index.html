Driving across Alaska

The Glenn Highway runs northeast from Anchorage through Palmer toward Glennallen. The Parks Highway connects Anchorage and Fairbanks past Denali. The Seward Highway follows Turnagain Arm south from Anchorage to Seward on the Kenai Peninsula. The Richardson Highway links Valdez to Fairbanks, and the Sterling Highway carries fishing traffic to Soldotna and Homer.

What the season does to Alaska's highways

Winter runs long. AK 511 posts surface conditions such as black ice and packed snow by highway, and the Seward Highway closes for avalanche control along Turnagain Arm. The Dalton Highway north of Fairbanks is mostly gravel, with services hundreds of miles apart. Moose on the road are a year-round hazard, worst in the dark months.

Before a Alaska road trip

  1. Check the map above for anything red on your route, then open AK 511 for the DOT's own detour and restriction text.
  2. Look at the road. The Alaska cameras show the pavement itself, and most carry the mile marker.
  3. Know how to say where you are. Dispatch, 911 and tow companies ask for the route, the direction and the mile marker. How to report your location on the highway.

Questions, answered

Is the highway closed in Alaska right now?

The map above shows every full closure AK 511 is publishing as a red marker, crashes in orange, and weather and hazard alerts in blue and purple. Closures the DOT has posted for a future date are listed as scheduled, and ramp and lane closures sit under the toggle so the list stays readable. It reloads every five minutes. If the feed cannot be reached the page says so instead of showing an empty map. For the official list, open AK 511.

Where do I check Alaska road conditions?

AK 511 is the official source, and dialing 511 in most states reaches the same information by phone. This page reads the same AK 511 feed and adds the Alaska cameras, the corridor and pass pages, and your own mile marker if you carry the app.

How often does this page update?

Every five minutes. MileCheck's server fetches the AK 511 feed, converts it to one format shared by all 50 states, and caches it for five minutes. Each alert also carries the time the DOT last updated it, shown in the list.
