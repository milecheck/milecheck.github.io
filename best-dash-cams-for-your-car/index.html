Questions, answered

Do I need a front and rear dash cam, or just front?

Front-only covers what happens ahead of you. A front-and-rear (2-channel) camera also covers rear-end collisions and parking-lot hit-and-runs — the more common claim disputes where a single front camera has nothing useful to show.

What happens to dash cam footage after a crash?

Most dash cams save footage to a memory card in a loop, overwriting old clips, but lock the file from a detected impact automatically so it isn't overwritten. Cloud-connected models also upload a copy, which survives even if the camera or card is damaged.

Does dash cam footage actually help with insurance claims?

It gives your insurer a timestamped, first-person record of what happened, which is far harder to dispute than two drivers' conflicting accounts. It doesn't decide fault by itself, but it removes a lot of the ambiguity insurers otherwise have to guess at.
