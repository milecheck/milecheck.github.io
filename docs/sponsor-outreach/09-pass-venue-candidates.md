# Pass pages: venue candidates, corrected targeting applied

`07-corridor-boards-venue-finding.md` found the real pattern: interstate truck-stop
chains (Pilot, Love's, TA, Petro) are the wrong target for a free-screen ask, because
they're all corporate — the request goes into a facilities/marketing approval chain
instead of to a local owner who can say yes on the spot. The 3 boards that exist
(Issaquah, Blaine, Ballard) all sit at **destination bottlenecks** — a pass, a border
crossing — where a small number of independently-owned businesses sit right at the
chokepoint everyone funnels through.

This pass searched all 9 mountain-pass pages on the site for that same shape of venue:
independent (not a national chain), currently operating, and sitting right at the base
of the climb where drivers already pull off — for gas, coffee, chains, or just to check
what's ahead before committing to the grade. Every candidate below was checked against
live reviews, business listings, or an active website to confirm it's real and still in
business — none of these are guesses.

## 1. Cabbage Hill (I-84) — Pendleton / Meacham, Oregon

**West side (Pendleton), primary: Arrowhead Travel Plaza**, 72485 OR-331, Pendleton, exit
216. Sits right at the base of the westbound climb, with a "Cabbage Hill" callout in its
own decor. One caveat worth knowing before reaching out: it's owned and operated by the
Confederated Tribes of the Umatilla Indian Reservation (CTUIR) under Wildhorse Resort &
Casino, not a single small-business owner. That's still a local decision-maker rather
than an out-of-state corporate office, but it's a tribal-enterprise approval, not a
one-person yes.

**East side (past the summit, La Grande): Gem Stop**, 2708 Island Ave, La Grande, exit
261 — a Shell/Chevron-branded independent station. Weaker fit geographically: La Grande
is past the summit descent into the Grande Ronde valley, not right at the base of the
grade the way Coles Corner or Kingvale sit. Meacham itself, at the actual summit, has no
currently operating business to approach — it's essentially a rest-area-only stop today.

## 2. Cajon Pass (I-15) — San Bernardino / Victorville / Hesperia, California

**Strong candidate: Outpost Cafe**, in Oak Hills near Hesperia, just off I-15 in the high
desert. Established 1961, independently owned, currently operating with 3,900+ reviews
averaging 4.5–4.6 stars. Sits right at the base of the southbound climb into Cajon Pass,
next to (not part of) a Pilot travel center — exactly the "everyone already stops here"
pattern. The pass's other historic independent landmark, the Summit Inn, burned down in
the 2016 Blue Cut Fire and no longer exists.

## 3. Donner Pass (I-80) — Truckee / Soda Springs, California

**Strong candidate: Kingvale Shell**, 53102 Donner Pass Rd, Soda Springs, exit 171.
This is literally inside the CHP-designated chain-control zone ("chain control now in
effect from Kingvale to Donner Lake Rd") — drivers stop here specifically because of pass
conditions, which is the exact overlap MileCheck sells. Small, independently-run station
(Shell-branded, not corporate Shell). The Kingvale Daily Grind cafe next door (53070
Donner Pass Rd) is a second option, but its current operating status wasn't confirmed —
check before including it in outreach.

## 4. Eisenhower Tunnel (I-70) — Silverthorne/Dillon/Frisco (west) and Georgetown/Idaho Springs (east), Colorado

**West side, strong candidate: Ski Country Auto Repair & Towing (Ski Country Shell)**,
1125 N Summit Blvd, Frisco. Family-owned and locally operated since 1971/1983 (owner
Larry Lewark), 4.7 stars, 24-hour towing — the kind of business that already deals with
every driver who got caught out by pass conditions.

**East side, strong candidate: Sinclair-Blackwell's**, 215 13th Ave, Idaho Springs.
Operated by Blackwell Oil Company, a local family fuel business (confirmed via BBB), not
a corporate Sinclair location. Currently operating with steady reviews.

## 5. The Grapevine / Tejon Pass (I-5) — Frazier Park / Lebec / Gorman, California

**North/summit side, strong candidate: Don's Gasoline**, 612 Monterey Trail, Frazier
Park. Small, clearly independent station (4.8 stars on GasBuddy), open early, serves the
mountain community directly — not a highway-only stop, so there's a standing local
relationship to build on.

**South side (LA-basin base of the climb): Gorman Union 76**, 49764 Gorman Post Rd,
Gorman. Incorporated locally in 2006; a 76-branded station is usually an independent
dealer, not a corporate-run site, though this wasn't independently confirmed beyond the
business registration. Reasonable second option if Don's doesn't pan out.

## 6. Parleys Summit (I-80) — Park City / Kimball Junction (west) and Wanship/Coalville (east), Utah

**West side, strong candidate: Bell's Silver Creek**, 7100 Silver Creek Rd, Park City,
exit 146. Operated by Bell Oil Company (confirmed via BBB) — sits right next to the
chain-up area that's served I-80 trucks near Parleys Summit for close to 20 years. Same
"drivers already stop here because of the pass" overlap as Kingvale.

**East side (past the summit): Sinclair, Wanship**, 2246 S UT-32 — described as
essentially the one operating business at that exit. Weaker pick than Bell's simply
because it's further from the actual summit/chain-up zone, but it's a real, currently
operating, single-location station worth knowing about as a fallback.

## 7. Siskiyou Summit (I-5) — Ashland, Oregon and Yreka/Weed, California

**South side, strong candidate: Siskiyou Food Mart & Valero**, 1802 Fort Jones Rd, Yreka.
Open 24 hours, independently owned and managed (contact listed as a named local operator
tied to Mt. Shasta Heating Fuels, not a corporate office) — Yreka is described as the last
real town before the climb into the summit, so this is the natural last-stop pattern.

**North side (Ashland): came up weak.** Exit 6, the historic base of the grade closest to
Mt. Ashland, has no gas station or store within 5 miles. The nearest options are several
miles into town on Siskiyou Blvd (e.g., a Shell station), which is more a downtown Ashland
business than a "drivers stop here because of the pass" venue. Worth Leah's own local
judgment call rather than more blind searching — this is the same diminishing-returns
pattern 07 flagged for interstate exits.

## 8. Stevens Pass (US-2) — Leavenworth (east) and Skykomish/Gold Bar (west), Washington

**East side, strong candidate: Coles Corner** — Coles Corner Market (gas, 15255 US-2) and
the 59er Diner (15361 US Highway 2), both independently operated, both currently open,
sitting together right on the highway about 15 miles from Leavenworth toward the pass.
This is close to a perfect match for the pattern: a motel, gas station, and diner cluster
that exists specifically because of through-traffic on the grade.

**West side, strong candidate: Sky Gas & Grocery**, 147 N 5th St, Skykomish (milepost
48.5 on US-2). Independent gas station and grocery, operating since 2006, currently open
6:30am–11pm. Reviews mention the difference in service since it stopped being a branded
Chevron — a good sign of genuine local ownership.

## 9. Vail Pass (I-70) — Vail / Frisco / Copper Mountain, Colorado

**Weaker overall.** Copper Mountain (west side, exit 195) turned up mostly chain options
(Conoco, Shell, 7-Eleven) plus City Pop, which is a candy/coffee shop, not a gas
station/diner in the target pattern. East Vail (exit 180) has no gas stations at all.

**Best available: Stop 'n Save (Feather Petroleum)**, West Vail, exit 173. Family-owned
and headquartered in Grand Junction since 1977, now run by second-generation owners — a
real Colorado family business, not a national chain. The honest caveat: it's a 16-store
regional operator, so a "yes" likely needs a regional decision rather than one person on
site, closer to a small chain than the single-owner pattern that made Issaquah/Blaine/
Ballard work. Still a meaningfully better fit than Pilot or Love's would be. Worth noting
Frisco (from the Eisenhower Tunnel section above) already has a stronger, more local
candidate and sits only a few miles from Vail Pass's own west side — Ski Country
Shell & Towing may be worth approaching as a Vail Pass venue too, not only an Eisenhower
one.

## Strongest first targets

1. **Coles Corner, Stevens Pass (WA)** — the cleanest match to the Issaquah/Blaine/
   Ballard pattern of anything found this pass: independent, verified, a motel-gas-diner
   cluster that exists because of the grade, and in MileCheck's home state where Leah can
   drive up and meet the owner in person.
2. **Kingvale Shell, Donner Pass (CA)** — sits inside CHP's own named chain-control zone,
   so the "drivers are here because of pass conditions" overlap is about as direct as it
   gets, and it's independently run.
3. **Outpost Cafe, Cajon Pass (CA)** — the strongest social proof of any candidate here
   (nearly 4,000 reviews, 65 years in business), confirming it's a genuine local landmark
   truckers and drivers already treat as a stop, not just a gas-station afterthought.

Bell's Silver Creek (Parleys Summit) and Ski Country Shell & Towing (Eisenhower Tunnel)
are close behind — both are chain-up-area businesses with a confirmed family-ownership
story, just slightly less air-tight than the top 3 on either the "right at the exact
bottleneck" or "verified currently operating" test.
