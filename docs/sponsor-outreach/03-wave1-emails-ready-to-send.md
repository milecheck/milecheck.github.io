# First sponsor sale — pricing to have in your head + two ready-to-send emails

## Pricing — two tiers (internal only; the site itself stays "email for pricing")

You have zero sponsors and zero case studies right now, so the goal of sale #1 isn't
maximum price — it's a signed yes you can point to for sale #2. The site sells by whole
topic family (e.g. sponsor "Cameras" = every state + city camera page, 70 pages), but
that's overkill reach for a hyper-local business like a single-state towing company —
so quote two tiers and let them pick:

- **Tier A — one page (their exact page):** $49/month. Just the page that's actually
  relevant to them — e.g. the Ohio cameras page for an Ohio towing company.
- **Tier B — every page in the topic, nationwide:** $99/month. All 45 state + 25 city
  camera pages (or the equivalent for corridors/passes/etc).

Both month-to-month, cancel anytime, founding rate while the program is new. Round
numbers, no hesitation when you say them out loud. Most local businesses will pick
Tier A — that's fine, it's still a real signed sponsor and a real case study.

**Added perk, costs nothing right now:** website sponsors get first option on corridor
board advertising (the physical screens at truck stops/travel centers) before it's
opened up publicly, once boards start going in. Boards have zero installs and zero
sales yet, so this promise is free to make — but it makes the $99/month ask stronger
today, and it turns every website sponsor into a warm lead for board sales later: a
business already paying you monthly is a far easier next call than a cold one. Track
who's a current sponsor somewhere simple (even a note in this file) so you know who to
call first when a board actually goes live near them.

Once you have one paying sponsor + real slot-view numbers to show as proof, raise the
rate for the next one. This number is a floor for the *first* close, not a lasting price.

**"First option," defined (for your own head, never spell this out to a prospect):**
first opportunity to buy corridor-board advertising in their area before it's opened up
publicly — not a guaranteed future price, and not a contractual right. If a prospect
asks you to define it, "you'll hear from us before anyone else can buy in" is accurate;
don't promise a locked price or a guaranteed slot.

Lead each email with ONE concrete number for the page that's actually relevant to them
(the $49 single-page tier) and stop there — don't mention the $99 nationwide tier in the
cold email at all. Every extra decision in the first message is a reason not to reply.
If they say yes, THEN say "by the way, if you want the whole network, that's $99" — the
upsell only makes sense once someone's already said yes to something.

Also say "planned" corridor-board screens, not "screens going into" — there are zero
installs right now, and the emails shouldn't read more concrete than that.

If Ohio or Atlanta's specific page has a real, impressive click number from Search
Console, put it in sentence two. Don't use the site-wide 7,610 as if that page itself
gets that traffic — I can't pull GSC's per-page numbers from here, so check the real
number yourself before adding one; it's fine to send without one too.

If a prospect asks for real traffic before committing, the live per-topic numbers are on
`/sponsor/#numbers` (Cameras family is the biggest — pull the current slot-views/clicks
row for Cameras before you reply to either email below, since both prospects sit on
camera pages).
