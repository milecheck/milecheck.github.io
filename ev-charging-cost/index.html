How much does it cost to charge an EV in each state?

Your mile marker on screen as you drive. Plus live alerts and cameras in the MileCheck app.App StoreGoogle Play

August 2026 prices · U.S. Energy Information Administration

Home electricity averaged 18.2¢ per kilowatt-hour in the U.S. in August 2026. For a car rated 30 kWh per 100 miles, that is about $5.45 to drive 100 miles, from $3.82 in Nevada to $14.15 in Hawaii.

How do you figure out what charging costs?

Multiply the price per kilowatt-hour by the kilowatt-hours your car uses. Your car's EPA rating is on its window sticker and on fueleconomy.gov, written as kWh per 100 miles. This page uses 30 as an example.

These are home prices. Public fast chargers usually cost more per kilowatt-hour, and some charge by the minute.

What does a 300-mile trip cost at home prices?

At the same 30 kWh per 100 miles, 300 miles comes to about $16.34 at the U.S. average of 18.2¢. It is $11.45 at Nevada prices and $42.46 at Hawaii prices. This is what the electricity costs if you charge at home, so it leaves out public charger fees.

Where did home electricity rise the most in a year?

Compared with a year earlier, the largest increases in cents per kilowatt-hour were Hawaii (+8.6¢), Maine (+4.0¢), New Hampshire (+3.2¢). Prices fell the most in Connecticut (-5.7¢). The "A year earlier" column in the table has each state's number.
