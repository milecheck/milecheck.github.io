The tire and chain rules in Washington

WSDOT's approved traction tires carry an M+S, All Season or mountain-and-snowflake marking and need enough tread. Check the tire itself. Studded tires may be used in Washington from November 1 through March 31. Studs do not substitute for chains when chains are required.

For a passenger AWD vehicle under 10,000 lb, Washington’s chain exception requires all wheels engaged, approved traction devices and chains carried for at least one set of drive tires. WSDOT advises anyone driving in high elevations or winter conditions to carry chains. Vehicles over 10,000 lb GVWR follow WSDOT’s separate commercial chain requirements.

Failing to obey a posted traction or chain requirement carries a $500 penalty under RCW 47.36.250. A tire that qualifies for a traction-tire sign is not a substitute for an installed chain when the sign requires chains.

Sources: WSDOT winter driving guide, WSDOT studded-tire dates, WAC 204-24-050, RCW 47.36.250.

Why Stevens needs a second look

The summit is 4,061 feet on US 2. US 2 over the summit closes for avalanche control and heavy snow, and the ski area sits at the top, so a chain requirement on a weekend can mean a slow line of traffic. The cameras and closures are on the Stevens Pass page.

Questions

Are chains required on Stevens Pass right now?

Read the live line at the top of this page, then WSDOT’s own pass report. The line can change within the hour.

Does AWD get me out of the chain requirement on Stevens Pass?

Only for the line that says chains are required on all vehicles except all-wheel drive, and only if all wheels are engaged, approved traction devices are fitted, and chains for at least one set of drive tires are carried. When the sign says chains are required on all vehicles, AWD must install them too. Source: WAC 204-24-050.
