Road data

What road data can I buy from MileCheck?

Two things. A live feed of state road alerts in one format. And records of when those alerts appeared and when they were no longer there.

The live feed is available now. The records of past alerts are offered only where the source owner has confirmed permission. We are asking agencies now.

What you get

Live feed. Available now.

Road alerts and conditions from state DOTs, through one API. Closures, crashes, construction and weather for all 50 states and British Columbia, Alberta and Manitoba. Coverage depends on what each agency publishes. Also the nearest mapped highway mile marker for a point, active wildfires, and Seattle drawbridge status. Test calls and field lists are on the API page.

Records of past alerts. Not yet available.

A row for each alert we detected, and each Seattle bridge lift. We check each feed about every 16 minutes. The row says when we first saw the alert and when we last saw it. Offered as a monthly file or through the API, once the source is confirmed. Pricing is below.

What the times mean

  • The times are when we saw an alert on a feed. They are not when it happened on the road.
  • "Last seen" is the last check that still showed the alert. The next check did not.
  • A crash leaving a feed does not prove the road was clear.
  • A feed can miss a bridge lift or split one into two. If a feed is down, we record nothing for that time.
  • Agencies post and remove alerts differently. Do not compare one state with another.
  • Confirm closures with the agency that runs the road. MileCheck is not the official source.

What we do

We put every state’s Department of Transportation data in one place, on your phone, in your car and in your browser.

Our values

  • No ads in the app. Our website is free to use, so it has a clearly marked sponsor slot to help pay for it, without pop-ups.
  • Road data, not your data. We collect road data from state DOTs, not data about you. You don’t need an account, your mile marker is calculated on your phone, your trip history stays there, and we never sell your data.
  • Only what’s needed. Weather sends your approximate location to the National Weather Service, route planning sends your location and destination to Google, and wildfire alerts send a location rounded to about 7 miles only if you turn them on. Sentry receives anonymous crash reports, we count feature use without location or identity, and Apple or Google handles subscriptions.
  • Built for real roads. Nearly every feature exists because someone on a real road asked for it.
