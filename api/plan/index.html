Data plan

Road data, as history files and an API

Drawbridge lift history is the closest to ready. It has one source, Seattle DOT, and one question left to settle. The crash and closure products each depend on every state agency separately.

MileCheck reads the live traffic feeds of all 50 state DOTs and three Canadian provinces. We also keep a log of when we first and last saw each record. We plan to offer that log, next to the live API.

This page says what is for sale, what is not, and why. The live API is on the API page.

Three history products we are preparing

None of these is on sale yet. Each is built from our own log, and each will be offered only for sources whose owners confirm in writing that we may license it. Until then, we are taking questions, not orders.

Drawbridge lift history

Bridge raises and lowers that MileCheck observed on the Seattle drawbridges Seattle DOT reports, with the length of each lift. Counts by hour, median and longest lift. Gaps in the city feed show up as gaps in the log.

Crash posting times

For each crash on a state DOT feed, when MileCheck first saw it and when it was no longer on a later check, by state, route and hour. We check about every 16 minutes. This is how long the agency kept it posted. It is not a measured clearance time, and every file says so.

Closure history by route

Closures and hazards by route, mile range and season. It separates a closure posted for 82 minutes from one posted for five days, which a "closed now" feed cannot do.

What the log looks like: the Ballard Bridge

In the 30 days to the evening of October 15, 2026, Pacific time, we observed 259 lifts. The middle lift lasted 243 seconds (about 4 minutes) and the longest 751 seconds. Most started between 9 AM and 3 PM. The city's feed can miss a lift or split one in two, so treat these as counts of what we observed. We will share record-level samples once the source terms are settled.

Crash posting times are published as state summaries on How long do crashes take to clear? That page covers 15,961 crashes in 36 states and provinces from September 25 to October 16, 2026. A history file would carry the same measure, per crash.

What we do not sell, and why

Any source whose terms we have not confirmed

A feed being public and keyless does not mean the agency allows resale. We check each agency's reuse terms and sell a state's history only when they are clear, or when the agency says yes in writing. Ask us which states are cleared today.

North Dakota

North Dakota DOT says its feed files are for processing only and not for public-facing use. We do not include it.

Camera images

Some camera images belong to third parties, and the agencies' terms differ. We do not sell camera images or our incident camera archive.

Official closure orders and legal restrictions

Our data follows the agency feeds and can be late or wrong. It is not a source for a closure order. Where it matters, use the agency's own site.

Anything about people

We sell road data. We do not collect or sell data about app users.

Our values

  • No ads in the app. Our website is free to use, so it has a clearly marked sponsor slot to help pay for it, without pop-ups.
  • Road data, not your data. We collect road data from state DOTs, not data about you. You don’t need an account, your mile marker is calculated on your phone, your trip history stays there, and we never sell your data.
  • Only what’s needed. Weather sends your approximate location to the National Weather Service, route planning sends your location and destination to Google, and wildfire alerts send a location rounded to about 7 miles only if you turn them on. Sentry receives anonymous crash reports, we count feature use without location or identity, and Apple or Google handles subscriptions.
  • Built for real roads. Nearly every feature exists because someone on a real road asked for it.
