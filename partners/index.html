Why it’s hard to build in-house
  • Every agency is different — 50 integrations, each its own auth, rate limits, and schema.
  • No common standard — no two states publish road data the same way.
  • State-by-state access — registration, API keys, and agency IT, times 50.
  • Continuous normalization — route names and mile formats change without notice.
  • Feed monitoring — without it, your app silently serves stale data.

The offer

Three products, each usable on its own.

Milepost dataset

Every interstate, US highway, and most state routes in all 50 states. Sourced from official DOT and GIS records, never crowdsourced, interpolated to one-tenth of a mile between markers.

  • Coverage — 50 states, continuously updated as routes are added or reclassified.
  • Fields — route ID, mile, latitude, longitude.
  • Delivery — structured dataset export, or continuous sync.
  • Also includes — a navigate-to-milepost primitive (a marker as a routing target, handed off to Google Maps, Apple Maps, or Waze) and the same data on CarPlay and Android Auto.
  • Limitation — positions are interpolated between physical signs, not individually GPS-surveyed.

Live conditions API

50 state DOTs and 3 Canadian provinces, each on a different upstream platform, normalized into one schema. Closures, construction, crashes, chain controls, and weather — tied to the exact mile marker.

  • Coverage — 50 states + 3 provinces, 19 upstream formats, one schema.
  • Also normalized — live CBP commercial border-crossing waits, and movable-bridge open/closed status (Seattle, Florida).
  • Refresh — matches each source's own posting cadence; every feed is monitored, not polled blind.
  • Unavailable-source behavior — ten states' live feeds don't carry crash data at all (Alaska, Arkansas, Hawaii, Kentucky, Maine, North Dakota, New Mexico, Oklahoma, South Dakota, West Virginia); the API says so rather than returning a silent zero.

Historical archive

Every state DOT and Canadian provincial feed (DriveBC, Alberta, Manitoba), saved on a schedule — a growing record of a source the agencies themselves purge within hours. No public archive like it exists today.

  • Collection interval — every two hours since September 2026, daily before that, back to mid-August.
  • Gaps — a jurisdiction's history starts the day its live integration went in, not before.
  • What it's produced — real crash-clearance times, bridge-raise duration histograms, and border-wait curves by hour and day, measured rather than estimated.
  • Delivery — a dated export, or a continuous sync, separate from the live API.

MileCheck solved this once, in production, for a consumer app live on both stores. Available to license today.
