What to check before you buy

Look for jumper cables rated for cold weather — cold saps battery power, and thin cables struggle exactly when you need them most. A reflective triangle or warning flares matter more in winter, when visibility is already worse. And whatever kit you pick, add what it's missing for your own drive: extra water, snacks, a phone charger, and any medication you'd need if you were stuck for a few hours. Questions, answered

What should be in a winter car emergency kit?

Jumper cables, a tow rope, a reflective triangle or warning flares, a flashlight, gloves, a tire pressure gauge, a first aid kit, and something warm — a blanket at minimum. A tire inflator and a thermal or emergency blanket are worth adding for winter specifically.

Do I need a big kit or is a small one enough?

A small kit covers the basics and takes up almost no trunk space, which means it actually stays in the car. A larger kit covers more scenarios but is heavier and bulkier. The best kit is the one you'll actually keep in the car year-round.

Should I add anything to a pre-made kit?

Check what's already in it, then add what's missing for your specific drive: extra water, snacks, a phone charger and battery pack, and any medication you'd need if you were stuck for a few hours.
