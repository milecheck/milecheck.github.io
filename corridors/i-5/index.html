The I-5 corridor

Washington · Oregon · California. Here's what to watch, and where the weather actually bites.

I-5 in Washington

From the Columbia River at Vancouver north through Kelso, Olympia, Joint Base Lewis-McChord (a chronic congestion choke point), Tacoma, Seattle, and Everett up to the Peace Arch crossing at Blaine. Snow is rare at sea level, but the Seattle–Tacoma stretch is one of the most congested corridors in the country — the cameras here are mostly about traffic, not weather.

I-5 in Oregon

Portland, Salem, Albany, Eugene, Roseburg, Grants Pass, Medford, and Ashland — then the climb to Siskiyou Summit at 4,310 feet, the highest point on the entire interstate. This is the winter wildcard: chains, spinouts, and full closures are common from November through March.

I-5 in California

Down from the Siskiyous past Mount Shasta and Redding, through Sacramento and the length of the Central Valley, over the Grapevine (Tejon Pass, 4,144 ft) into Los Angeles, and on to San Diego. The Grapevine is the other notorious closure point — snow and high wind shut it down several times each winter.

What is the highest point on I-5?

Siskiyou Summit in southern Oregon, at 4,310 feet — the highest elevation on all 1,381 miles of Interstate 5. It's marked with a ▲ on the map above.

Which parts of I-5 close in winter?

Almost always Siskiyou Summit in Oregon and the Grapevine in California — both can close for snow, ice, or high wind. Watch the storms behind them on the road weather map.
