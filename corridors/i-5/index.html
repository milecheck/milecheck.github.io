The I-5 corridor, state by state

Interstate 5 is the West Coast's main artery — one continuous route from the Canadian border to the Mexican border. Here's what to watch on each stretch, and where the weather actually bites.

I-5 in Washington MP 0 – 276

From the Columbia River at Vancouver north through Kelso, Olympia, Joint Base Lewis-McChord (a chronic congestion choke point), Tacoma, Seattle, and Everett up to the Peace Arch border crossing at Blaine. Snow is rare at sea level, but the Seattle–Tacoma stretch is one of the most congested corridors in the country — the live cameras here are mostly about traffic, not weather.

I-5 in Oregon MP 0 – 308

Portland, Salem, Albany, Eugene, Roseburg, Grants Pass, Medford, and Ashland — then the climb to Siskiyou Summit at 4,310 feet, the highest point on the entire interstate. This is the winter wildcard: chains, spinouts, and full closures are common here from November through March. If any part of I-5 is going to be closed, it's usually this pass or the Grapevine.

I-5 in California MP 0 – 796

Down from the Siskiyous past Mount Shasta and Redding, through Sacramento and the length of the Central Valley, over the Grapevine (Tejon Pass, 4,144 ft) into Los Angeles, and on to San Diego. The Grapevine is the other notorious closure point — snow and high wind shut it down several times each winter. In the valley, summer heat and dust are the bigger camera stories.

I-5 questions, answered

The live map above shows active closures and incidents as red and orange markers, straight from WSDOT, ODOT, and Caltrans. For a full US view, see the road closures map. Winter closures on I-5 most often hit Siskiyou Summit in southern Oregon and the Grapevine north of Los Angeles.

What's the highest point on I-5?

Siskiyou Summit in southern Oregon, at 4,310 feet. It regularly requires chains or closes during winter storms — the single most weather-sensitive spot on the route. Check it on the live map before you head south, or track your exact mile marker over the pass with the app.

Which parts of I-5 close in winter?

Two chronic trouble spots: Siskiyou Summit near the Oregon–California border, and the Grapevine (Tejon Pass) between the Central Valley and Los Angeles. Both can close for snow, ice, or high wind, sometimes with little warning. Watch the storms feeding these closures on the road weather map, and closures as they happen on the closures map.
