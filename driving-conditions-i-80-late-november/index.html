Holiday travel

What are driving conditions like on I-80 in late November?

Late November on I-80 can bring chain controls in the Sierra, traction requirements in Utah and wind restrictions in Wyoming. Conditions can change by state and by road segment. Check each state's current report before your Thanksgiving trip.

Facts checked October 2026. Every number links to its source; prices and rules change, so confirm the day you travel.

Which stretches of I-80 need a closer look?

Start in California at Donner Summit, where I-80 crosses the Sierra. Caltrans can require chains or traction devices during storms, and its published guidance says the I-80 speed limit drops to 30 mph when chain controls are in effect. Check the Donner Summit page and Caltrans QuickMap before leaving.

In Nevada, check for posted controls before approaching a mountain segment. Nevada's chain-control restrictions are activated with conditions, not by a fixed late-November calendar date. The current restrictions appear on NV Roads.

Farther east, Parleys Canyon in Utah is a designated I-80 traction-law area during severe winter weather. Review the Parleys Summit page along with UDOT Traffic.

Southern Wyoming needs a separate check for wind as well as winter road conditions. WYDOT identifies the Arlington to Elk Mountain stretch on its I-80 condition reports. Its wind notices can restrict high-profile vehicles or vehicles below a posted gross vehicle weight threshold. WYDOT also uses rolling closures, extending a closure back to a town with more services. Do not assume the closure ends at the first location shown on a route overview.

Check Ohio through New Jersey reports for the eastern trip.

What driving rules apply in late November?

California chain controls have no fixed seasonal start date. At R-1, most vehicles need chains, with an exception for qualifying passenger vehicles and light trucks with snow tires on at least two drive wheels. Those exempt vehicles must carry chains. At R-2, four-wheel-drive and all-wheel-drive vehicles with snow-tread tires on all four wheels can proceed without installed traction devices, but they must carry them. R-3 requires traction devices on every vehicle. Caltrans says roads are often closed before R-3 is imposed. California permits studded tires from November 1 through April 30.

Nevada requires chains or snow tires in posted chain-control areas. Authorities may restrict entry based on a vehicle's condition. In Utah, Class 1 traction restrictions cover vehicles over 12,000 pounds gross vehicle weight. Class 2 covers all vehicles. Under Class 2, four-wheel-drive and all-wheel-drive vehicles need M+S or three-peak mountain-snowflake tires on all wheels. Two-wheel-drive vehicles need three-peak mountain-snowflake tires on all wheels. Chains or snow socks on drive wheels are accepted. Utah permits studded tires October 15 through March 31.

Wyoming applies two chain-law levels when conditions warrant. Level 1 allows chains, adequate snow tires or all-wheel drive. Level 2 requires chains or all-wheel drive with qualifying mud-and-snow or all-weather tires. Commercial vehicles at Level 2 need chains on at least two drive wheels at opposite ends of the same axle. WYDOT's No Unnecessary Travel means the road is open but travel is not recommended. Extreme Blow Over Risk means reported gusts of at least 60 mph and is directed at high-profile and light vehicles. Check the exact posting, especially if you are towing or driving an RV.

What happened in recent late Novembers?

From November 28 through 30, 2025, a Thanksgiving weekend storm brought the highest snow totals along I-80 and across the Upper Midwest, according to NOAA's regional climate summary. Iowa State Patrol responded to more than 100 crashes that weekend. Chicago recorded 8.4 inches of snow on November 29. That is a regional storm record, not a measurement for every mile of I-80.

No Thanksgiving-week closure over Donner Summit or in Wyoming was verified for 2021 through 2025.

When is Thanksgiving traffic heavier?

The national AAA 2025 Thanksgiving forecast projected 81.8 million people traveling at least 50 miles between November 25 and December 1, 2025. At least 73 million were expected to travel by car. These are national 2025 forecast figures, not an I-80 traffic count or a 2026 prediction.

In that 2025 forecast, INRIX expected the most congestion before Thanksgiving on Tuesday from noon to 9 PM and Wednesday from 11 AM to 8 PM, based on local metro time. Sunday was expected to be busy from 11 AM to 8 PM. Those windows describe last year's national travel forecast. For Thanksgiving on Thursday, November 26, 2026, check the updated forecast when published and allow for local road restrictions regardless of travel day.

Where can I check I-80 conditions before leaving?

Check California QuickMap, Nevada's NV Roads, Utah's UDOT Traffic and Wyoming's WYOROAD. These state sources cover the western crossings and posted restrictions. For Wyoming, read the vehicle-specific wind notice rather than only the general roadway status.

For the central and eastern route, use Nebraska 511, Iowa 511, Getting Around Illinois, Indiana 511, Ohio's OHGO, Pennsylvania 511 and New Jersey 511. MileCheck's I-80 corridor page and chain-control guide provide additional route context. Check conditions before you leave and check again while parked if your plans change.
