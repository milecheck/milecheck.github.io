What is a runaway truck ramp?

A runaway truck ramp is an emergency road built to stop a truck that can no longer slow down safely on a steep downhill grade. The Federal Highway Administration also calls it a truck escape ramp.

These ramps take an out-of-control vehicle away from moving traffic. You may see signs for them on mountain passes or other highways with long descents.

Why would a truck need a runaway ramp?

A heavy truck gains speed as it travels downhill. Using the brakes too much on a long descent can create excess heat. That heat can reduce braking power, a condition called brake fade. In a severe case, a driver may no longer be able to control the truck's speed.

Professional drivers are trained to choose a suitable low gear before descending a steep grade. A truck escape ramp is an emergency measure when a vehicle still becomes uncontrollable. It is not an ordinary exit or a place to stop for a break.

How does a runaway truck ramp stop a truck?

Many ramps have a deep bed of loose gravel. The gravel increases resistance as the truck rolls into it, helping bring the vehicle to a stop. Some ramps also slope uphill, so gravity helps reduce speed.

Not every ramp works the same way. Highway agencies may use uphill gravity ramps, sand beds, or other stopping systems. The design depends on the grade, available space, and road layout.

The FHWA says escape ramps should be placed where they can intercept runaway vehicles before dangerous curves or populated areas. Their approaches are designed to be visible and reachable by a driver who has trouble controlling the vehicle.

Who can use a truck escape ramp?

It is reserved for runaway vehicles, usually large trucks with serious braking problems. It is not a scenic pullout, parking space, or shortcut. Federal traffic-sign guidance calls for warnings ahead of escape ramps and signs discouraging other drivers from entering.

If you see a runaway truck, give it space. Do not drive into or block an escape ramp. A truck using one may need the entire entrance and stopping area.

Where are these ramps found?

They are generally installed on steep downgrades where an uncontrolled heavy vehicle could pose a serious risk. They may be near the bottom of a hill or before a sharp bend. Not every mountain road has one, so drivers should not assume a ramp will be available.
